## Re-stacking your four windows with real tidal geometry

This is where your order needs a tweak. In tidal physics both the sub-lunar point and the anti-lunar point produce a bulge outward, away from Earth's center.

**1. Sun Up + Moon Up - new moon spring**
Both luminaries overhead. Direct pulls both upward, tidal vectors both outward. Maximum reduction in apparent weight. This is your Air / peak buoyancy case, and you have it right.

**2. Sun Down + Moon Down - new moon spring on the night side**
Both below your feet. Direct pulls now add downward, but the Earth center is being pulled down more strongly than you are, so the net tidal effect is still outward, still a weight reduction. Gravimetrically it is identical to case 1. It is not maximum compression, it is the other half of the same spring tide.

**3. Sun Down + Moon Up or Sun Up + Moon Down - full moon spring**
One above, one below, opposite sides. Still syzygy, still a combined outward tide. Also maximum lightness, about the same as 1 and 2.

**4. Quarter moons - Moon at horizon, 90 degrees from Sun**
This is actually the heaviest tidal state. When the moon is on your horizon, the tidal vector points inward, slightly increasing apparent weight. That is neap tide.

In short: up + up and down + down are the same family. The density switch you are feeling between heavy night and light day is not coming from gravity.
